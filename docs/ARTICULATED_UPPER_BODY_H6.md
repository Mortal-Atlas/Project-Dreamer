# H6 Supported articulated upper body

## Mechanism

The H6 condition uses the existing household Ball and the H5 contact, release, and settled-commit gates. A distinct household policy selects the articulated condition and is recorded in the same attempt ledger. H5 remains the carriage reference. In H6 a fixed torso proxy supports a rigid upper arm, a rigid forearm and the existing palm and opposed pads. The shoulder has three actively driven rotational axes with native range limits. The elbow has one physical hinge axis built from a locked-translation configurable joint, with engineered torque stops at 6–151° and planned flexion of 9–148°. The wrist actively tracks orientation in three axes within the native joint cone. Independent forearm pronation and a multi-joint finger articulation remain open. The fingers retain the H5 opposing prismatic drives. All connections have locked translation. Only the fixed torso takes external support.

The calibration multiplies the two birth-seeded upper limb lengths by 1.43 for the ball bench, with separate dimensions for Arthur and Alice. The selected default calibration is age three developmental days. It describes the reduced bench and does not resize the learner's persisted body or visual avatar. The upper-arm and forearm masses scale from 0.43 kg per 0.32 m and 0.31 kg per 0.26 m. The palm is 0.12 kg. Colliders define each native center of mass and moments of inertia. Distal segments carry gravity. Torque-limited controllers apply reactions to connected segments. Their effective inertia and 5 ms update provide implicit damping. Shoulder, elbow and wrist torque bounds are 13, 8 and 3 N m. Link rotation and angular velocity, reconstructed hinge flexion, and known rig gravity compensation drive the motors. No grip-object mass metadata enters the arm controller.

The solver proposes an actual elbow and wrist configuration each 50 ms. A lateral and a high elbow direction can construct different solutions for the same target. A prior torso or blocker contact of at least 10 ms in one update can select a higher outward candidate once. The candidate and actual trajectory appear in the trace. The guide requests wrist coordinates from the H5 bench task or a researcher-specified probe. The wrist pose and geometry used in the feedback are idealized proprioceptive readings. Live visual hand-eye error and adapted estimates of the body are separate dependencies.

The upper arm is trimmed near the shoulder mount and collides with the torso. The forearm and distal hand also collide with it. Directly adjacent upper arm–forearm, forearm–palm, and palm–digit attachment pairs are excluded. Nonadjacent upper arm–hand contact remains active. Report native contact impulse, substep duration and maximum proxy intersection. Arm garments and skin meshes are independent of these proxies.

## Reach, load and contact

Eight of 12 development reaches and 16 of 22 scored reaches entered the 28 mm band for five consecutive ticks. At tick 80, 12 of 22 remained inside the band. The high elbow qualified 9 of 11 and stayed inside for all nine. The lateral elbow qualified 7 of 11 and stayed inside for three. Median final error was 10.0 mm with the higher elbow and 41.6 mm with the lateral elbow. Several lateral reaches qualified and drifted afterward. Both approaches qualified on the opposite-side and opposite-waist targets. The high approach reached the independently calibrated shorter Alice target, and the lateral approach did not qualify. No scored reach produced a monitored elbow excursion beyond the diagnostic stop tolerance.

In the matched wrist-load window, the peak position error with the higher elbow rose from 10.3 to 15.3 mm, while shoulder peak torque rose from 2.63 to 2.85 N m. The lateral branch moved from 63.1 to 105.8 mm peak error during the same window. The paired no-load and loaded variants use identical anatomy and target commands. At −23° requested yaw, final orientation error was about 5.2° for each approach.

The blocked target failed both approaches. With the contact signal repaired, they measured 3.48 and 3.74 s of obstacle contact and each triggered one actual elbow revision. The revision did not clear the blocker. In the outside-envelope test the upper arm touched the torso for up to 2.55 s. Maximum measured torso intersection in the primary set was 0.091 mm. The supported Ball trials measured zero torso contact. Binding adjustments during development removed spurious penetration and joint impulses from the initial invalid setup. ## Loaded transfer and persistence

The H5 carriage completed all three matched bench transfers. Both articulated approaches gripped the Ball. The lateral elbow approach failed the sustained-clearance gate for both learners, so its scheduled load never fired. The higher elbow approach lifted, held for all 12 qualified ticks and transferred weight to the table in all three comparisons. It received all five scheduled load ticks in Arthur's disturbance case, yet none of the three articulated attempts completed the full sequence.

Arthur’s higher approach reached 263 mm peak clearance without added load and 260 mm under load. At release entry the receiving table carried 4.15 N and 4.02 N respectively, including the transient downward transfer force. On those trials the Ball drifted 77.8 mm and 66.3 mm horizontally during the release window. The H5 gate permits less than 18 mm. Both settled on the table and kept the actual pose after failing that gate. Alice qualified the release window, but the Ball subsequently rolled off the target and came to rest on the floor. The early support cue alone is therefore insufficient for stable placement. ## Verification and performance

Release .NET passed 123 tests and Unity Edit Mode passed 124. Unity 6000.3.19f1 DX12 verified the articulated chain in live hold and passive replay. The two H5 settled checkpoints restored byte-for-byte, including the earlier 1.52 m unintended displacement. Arthur completed the H2 retained grasp and returned to Living. The H4 browser repeated its measured load, reposition, placement and passive replay.

On the held-out reach bench, the native controller averaged 0.047 ms and PhysX averaged 0.487 ms per 50 ms tick. The full H6 household attempts spent 0.579–0.692 ms in native physics and 0.059–0.067 ms in motor commands per tick. The live CPU camera submission capture took 1.188 ms and replay 0.986–1.911 ms. Their settled world checkpoints were 23,192 and 23,108 bytes.

Their four-person medians were 57,290 and 56,673 ticks/s, with overlapping ranges. Both used 2,285,960 measured allocated bytes and a 44,878-byte checkpoint in the longer comparison.
