# H4 tactile exploration and controlled placement

## Frozen comparison protocol

H4 preserves the H3 controls and uses the same supported two-guide PhysX hand. H3 scores and telemetry remain on their original protocol. H4 comparisons share a laterally actuated carriage and a marked destination on the native table. The object is a free rigidbody throughout an attempt. Ten 5 ms physics substeps feed each 50 ms decision tick. H2 household practice keeps its original mechanics and save format. [H3 measurements](PHYSICAL_HAND_H3.md) [H2 household evidence](PHYSICAL_DEVELOPMENT_H2.md)

The H4 motor policy consumes pad duration, normal force, tangential movement, and contact centroids relative to the two pads. It begins with a light probe when the starting cap is fresh. Three bilateral quiet ticks admit a lift and bring a low cap up to 0.90 N. Persistent unilateral or missing contact, asymmetric force and distal contact can trigger up to two open, back-off, reposition and reclose cycles. The engineered candidates shift approach depth, height, lateral alignment and pitch from sensed pad evidence. Force increases from slip or lost opposition stay bounded by 3.2 N. Uncertain contact suspends upward and transport commands. Three ticks without loaded opposition cause early lowering. Finger guides still share a symmetric closing command. Finger coordination, yaw, thumb rotation and independent effort remain prerequisites for broader geometries.

The evaluator supplies the nominal approach origin and destination. Shape, mass, friction, offset, disturbance, center of mass, object pose and angular motion remain outside the tactile policy input. The fixture phase supervisor uses instrumented clearance, non-pad contacts and table support to score and phase the task. Table support records load and center of contact, and calibrates its load gate from the settled object. The current placement supervisor also uses instrumented object velocity to confirm transfer and settling. A future household path must replace those instrumented quantities with observed support and visually estimated hand, object and destination poses.

The primary endpoint is the entire physical sequence. The rig must acquire opposed contact, clear the table by 35 mm, hold for 12 consecutive ticks (0.60 s) with no non-pad strike, and transport to the destination. With bilateral contact still present, table support must reach 45% of the measured settled load while vertical speed is below 60 mm/s. The support centroid must lie within 24 mm laterally and 39 mm in depth of the destination center, 60 mm to the left. Release requires five consecutive ticks with both pads disengaged, table support above 50% of measured load, object underside within 4 mm of the table, speed below 50 mm/s, support still inside the target, and horizontal post-release drift below 18 mm. Existing joint bounds and 4 mm maximum penetration apply. In a trial without a verified hold, measured release can describe resettling near the origin. That trial cannot score full success.

Measure phase transitions, bilateral and unilateral contact durations, non-pad contacts, contact-loss edges, lift, longest hold and placement separately. Keep force, slip duration and magnitude, local contact position, joint travel and velocity, wrist and object orientation, applied load, corrective sensing and next-tick cap application, reposition events, object displacement, linear and wrist work and native compute time. A trial falls below the support surface if clearance reaches less than −25 mm. A cap change observed at tick T first affects commands on tick T+1.
