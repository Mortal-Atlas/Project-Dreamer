# H4 tactile exploration and controlled placement

## Frozen comparison protocol

H4 preserves the H3 controls and uses the same supported two-guide PhysX hand. H3 scores and telemetry remain on their original protocol. H4 comparisons share a laterally actuated carriage and a marked destination on the native table. The object is a free rigidbody throughout an attempt. Ten 5 ms physics substeps feed each 50 ms decision tick. H2 household practice keeps its original mechanics and save format. [H3 measurements](PHYSICAL_HAND_H3.md) [H2 household evidence](PHYSICAL_DEVELOPMENT_H2.md)

The H4 motor policy consumes pad duration, normal force, tangential movement, and contact centroids relative to the two pads. It begins with a light probe when the starting cap is fresh. Three bilateral quiet ticks admit a lift and bring a low cap up to 0.90 N. Persistent unilateral or missing contact, asymmetric force and distal contact can trigger up to two open, back-off, reposition and reclose cycles. The engineered candidates shift approach depth, height, lateral alignment and pitch from sensed pad evidence. Force increases from slip or lost opposition stay bounded by 3.2 N. Uncertain contact suspends upward and transport commands. Three ticks without loaded opposition cause early lowering. Finger guides still share a symmetric closing command. Finger coordination, yaw, thumb rotation and independent effort remain prerequisites for broader geometries.

The evaluator supplies the nominal approach origin and destination. Shape, mass, friction, offset, disturbance, center of mass, object pose and angular motion remain outside the tactile policy input. The fixture phase supervisor uses instrumented clearance, non-pad contacts and table support to score and phase the task. Table support records load and center of contact, and calibrates its load gate from the settled object. The current placement supervisor also uses instrumented object velocity to confirm transfer and settling. A future household path must replace those instrumented quantities with observed support and visually estimated hand, object and destination poses.

The primary endpoint is the entire physical sequence. The rig must acquire opposed contact, clear the table by 35 mm, hold for 12 consecutive ticks (0.60 s) with no non-pad strike, and transport to the destination. With bilateral contact still present, table support must reach 45% of the measured settled load while vertical speed is below 60 mm/s. The support centroid must lie within 24 mm laterally and 39 mm in depth of the destination center, 60 mm to the left. Release requires five consecutive ticks with both pads disengaged, table support above 50% of measured load, object underside within 4 mm of the table, speed below 50 mm/s, support still inside the target, and horizontal post-release drift below 18 mm. Existing joint bounds and 4 mm maximum penetration apply. In a trial without a verified hold, measured release can describe resettling near the origin. That trial cannot score full success.

Measure phase transitions, bilateral and unilateral contact durations, non-pad contacts, contact-loss edges, lift, longest hold and placement separately. ## Failure diagnosis

The H3 results are preserved byte-for-byte. In H3 the added-load feedback trial went from 30.1 mm clearance just before the intervention to 9.1 mm two control ticks later. Both pads lost measured contact the following tick, before the controller applied its 3.2 N bound. Continued upward control with no opposing contact preceded the off-table loss. The sequence supports late compensation for this case. It does not identify gains independently of starting force, friction and orientation. A separate H4 development sphere with a 0.40 N disturbance passed both old references, so the failure is conditional on the setup.

The H3 offset capsule rotated and moved before any hand contact. Its H4 diagnostic analogue rotated 120.17° while settling in both matched reference trials and neither lifted. The H3 upright low-friction capsule rotated during lift while the fixed arms maintained opposing contact, which rules out simple lack of opposition as the only mechanism. Its separate 0.40-friction development analogue held and released under fixed grip on the original task, and reached lift but failed the sequence under H3 feedback. During H4 transport the original fixed policy put weight on the destination for this development capsule but failed the subsequent release. Orientation, stable contact and release each require their own gate.

The H3 fixed grip lifted the lightly yawed box 58.7 mm with very asymmetric pad forces, yet never entered the accepted hold. The H3 feedback reference reached only unilateral contact on that case. The similarly yawed centered H4 development box passed the original sequence with both policies. Translating that H4 box 19 mm laterally and 22 mm in depth caused the H3 feedback reference to fail. During the H4 task the same geometry caused asymmetric or distal contact, physical repositioning and failed placement on two tactile development attempts. The evidence supports a contact-position dependency. It cannot uniquely separate wrist stiffness, guide limits, pad geometry and approach alignment. No actuator fault detector or curvature estimator was evaluated.

The initial six diagnosis cases ran before controller tuning. The displaced-box case was then added to development to test contact location directly. The committed diagnosis command was subsequently rerun on all seven cases, with the historical H3 outcomes left intact. All six arms failed the offset capsule. Tactile tried two positions, reached bilateral opposition and entered lift at the 3.2 N bound. It lost contact and lowered without a qualified hold. The repeated repositioning improved initial contact on this object without completing the task. On the displaced held-out box, Tactile shifted forward about 4 mm and changed commanded pitch by 1.46°. It placed the box after 110 ticks. The same-cap force-only arm placed it after 88 ticks and matched Tactile on every binary outcome. These conditions provide no evidence of a success advantage from the engineered geometry proposals. Fixed also matched full-sequence success and used more digit effort on average.

All six arms received the full 0.58 N load. H3 feedback on this case kept a 1.06 N cap through the first four loaded ticks, then lost the sequence and the object later fell off the table. Retained H4 detected the needed cap change on the fourth loaded tick and applied 1.95 N on the fifth. Reset H4 responded on the first loaded tick and applied 1.55 N on the second. Both H4 variants placed the object, as did fixed, scalar and force-only. These observations measure 50 ms update ordering. They do not isolate response gain from the different starting caps and motor phases. All policies passed the other loaded sphere and the impulse case.
